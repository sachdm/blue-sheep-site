The episode, beat by beat

01
Golden hour, Toronto — 5:00PM. Office towers emptying into the Friday street. Patios going up. "It's Friday, and all is renewed."
the world, and the promise it makes
02
Vincent, still at the desk — 5:30PM. Everyone's packing up. Vincent (25) stares at a dark phone, hair holding the shape of the morning, eyes as red as his tie. Then: UNKNOWN.
a man waiting for permission
03
Corey, outside the stadium — 5:45PM. Blue jersey, half his face painted, checking a phone nobody's texting. Shorty #1 arrives and immediately calls him on his height.
the same waiting, different costume
04
Alone on the floor — 6PM. "Reba!" Nothing. The office is dead, chairs tucked, screens off. He puts headphones in and works anyway.
nobody is watching — which is the point
05
The joint in the park — Shorty pulls a joint out of a tiny bag. "You hit first, or you'll be covered in my lip gloss."
Corey's evening costs him nothing
06
The texts start — 7PM. Eric's messages stack up. Sayflair may be liquidating. Vincent's face sours, and he stands.
THE INCITING INFORMATION
07
The phone call — A friend on speaker: the Star won't report it, the client list won't drop till Monday, none of it's actionable. "Nothing, nothing till Monday..." — and then, quietly, "I'm gonna call Paul."
he decides to move while everyone else waits
08
Beer instead of a vodka tonic — 7:30PM. Corey hands Shorty the wrong drink and lies about it. She drinks it anyway. "This is actually okay."
two people agreeing not to mind
09
The eleventh floor — 7:45PM. Vincent rides down, waits with his phone as an alibi, slips through a door behind a leaving assistant, and walks past dark desks to the one lit boardroom.
THE BREAK-IN — sixty seconds of pure nerve
10
"Gentlemen — I have heard some disturbing news on the 15th floor" — Four men, laptops, dossiers. Gerald (35) checks his watch: fifteen minutes. "How did you even get in here?" / "Because I wanted to get in here."
the pitch begins as trespass
11
The ask — "I want to represent you. You would be my first and only account." Gerald's answer is a no dressed as kindness — your mother, corporate laws, this isn't a Scorsese film.
REFUSAL
12
The speech — Vincent turns from Gerald to the room. You'll be picked up by someone with a handful of clients like you, buttered up, promised funding on an old project, and back to being a data centre in two months.
THE TURN — he stops selling himself and starts telling the truth
13
"...or you could grow some balls like me, buddy" — And the exit line: "I'm going up, guaranteed. You? Not so much."
the closer
14
Monday, 7PM, TinTin — Gerald laughs, leans back, and tells him the boss will want to hear it directly. Vincent books a steakhouse and keeps the frown glued on.
VICTORY — held completely still
15
"I'm exactly where I want to be, doing what I want to do" — Vincent exits the office.
the thesis, said by a man who just risked everything to make it true

The structural note: Corey's thread is beautifully observed and currently does no work. Three options — converge the threads (Corey is at Sayflair, or knows someone who is), sharpen the contrast (cut Corey's material by half so it reads as counterpoint rather than subplot), or hold him for the series and cut him almost entirely from the pilot. Right now the pilot is Vincent's, and the script hasn't decided that yet.
Recurrences worth protecting: the dark phone (4×, both men), the time stamps (every scene, 5:00 → 7:45), the necktie Vincent keeps touching, and "going up" — literal floors and the whole ambition.
