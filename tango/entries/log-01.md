# TWIST THE TANGO — Log 01 · First Read

**Episode 1, pilot draft v1 · 13 pp**

---

## What's working

**The boardroom sequence is the show.** Pages 8 to 13 — the break-in, the pitch, the refusal, the speech, the win — are the best writing on the Blue Sheep slate right now. Vincent talks his way into a room he has no right to be in and then, when the no lands, stops selling himself and starts describing exactly what will happen to these men if they take the safe option. It's specific, it's cruel, and it's correct. Gerald's turn from dismissal to interest is earned in one page.

**The time stamps are a real structural device.** 5:00, 5:30, 5:45, 6, 7, 7:30, 7:45 — one Friday evening, cut between two lives, tightening as it goes. It gives a talky pilot a clock.

**Corey's material is beautifully observed.** The height argument, the joint, the wrong drink and the lie about it, the man eating a hotdog with both hands at the urinal. Nothing in it is wasted as writing.

## What it needs

**Corey does no plot work.** He's a life running parallel to Vincent's, not a story braided into it. Three ways out: converge the threads, cut his material by half so it reads as counterpoint, or hold him almost entirely for the series. The pilot is Vincent's — the script hasn't admitted that yet.

**The ending is unconfirmed.** The draft stops at a scene 9 marker with nothing under it. If it ends on Vincent walking out of that office, that's a strong ending. If pages are missing, they're the ones that matter.

**Vincent's firm is unnamed** while Sayflair, Canacor and TinTin all are. Name it — it's where he's trying to climb.

**No series bible exists.** For a short, the script is the application. For a pilot, the bible is. What is episode 2, and what is the season? That document, not another draft, is what makes this fundable.
