Tax credits: not on this film

Take the $10,000 from OAC. Skip the credits on this short.

OAC makes projects that seek tax credits ineligible, so it is one or the other. On this budget the credits would likely come to less than the grant, and they need a distribution deal the film does not have. The full reasoning is further down.

Deadline: your call this week

October 8 only if NOMAD gives up the slot. Otherwise March 2027, and shoot in September.

OAC takes one application per artist per deadline, and the slate plan gives October 8 to NOMAD. The March route moves the shoot to real early autumn, which also fixes the script's season problem.

Which deadline

OAC results arrive about four and a half months after the deadline, and nothing can start before them. That one rule sets the whole calendar.

Route A · only if NOMAD steps aside
Deadline Oct 8, 2026, 1 p.m. ET · 14 days from today
  1. Submit Oct 7. The portal slows on the day.
  2. TAC results in late December.
  3. OAC results around late February 2027.
  4. Pre-production in March, shoot in April.

Cost: two weeks to write it, and NOMAD moves to Canada Council only. An April shoot still contradicts the script's autumn, so the exteriors need a rewrite.

Route B · recommended if NOMAD keeps Oct 8
Deadline around mid-March 2027 · 2026's was March 17
  1. TAC results in late December. Hold everything.
  2. Submit to OAC in March with a finished shooting draft.
  3. OAC results around early August 2027.
  4. Shoot in mid-September, in real early-autumn light.

Cost: the festival year moves from 2027 to 2028. Confirm with TAC that a September 2027 shoot sits inside their grant period.

Draft answers

Written to OAC's three assessment areas: artistic merit, impact and viability. Paste each into the matching question in Nova and trim to its character counter. Highlighted brackets are facts only you can fill in.

The budget, in council shape

Full value on both sides. In-kind appears as an expense and as revenue, so the cash need is visible. No tax-credit line anywhere, because that alone would make the application ineligible.

With OAC in the mix, deferred fees drop from $8,000 to about $2,000. Say so in the application, and say deferrals are the first thing paid if more money lands. Juries read unpaid artists as a viability risk.

Can Not Another Word qualify for tax credits?

In principle a short can qualify for both the federal credit (CPTC, certified by CAVCO) and the Ontario credit (OFTTC, certified by Ontario Creates). In practice this one fails four tests as it stands, and the grants it depends on would shrink the credit anyway.
