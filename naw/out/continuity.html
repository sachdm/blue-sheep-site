Not Another Word · Form and Continuity

One afternoon to the next morning, in one apartment, one pub and one street. The form is a closed loop: the last scene puts every opening object back where it was, without the man. Below the form, every event in order, what each prop and costume is doing in each scene, and the continuity problems to fix before the board locks.

The form

The story clock starts at the 3:00 PM alarm, at the top of the dial, and runs clockwise. Times after the alarm are estimates from the script. The red chords join each opening image to the closing image that answers it.

A ritual, played once, running down

The first movement is the routine: alarm, Chivas, radio, cigarette, the Dragon, the patio. Every later scene takes one piece of it away. By the interview the Dragon is empty and the drink is almost gone.

Out and back

The only trip outside the apartment. The pub is where the world stops pretending, and the study is where he quits everything, too late to change a paper already printed.

The interview is a second ritual

The crew rebuilds his living room into a set and runs their own routine on him: questions, the paper, the pulse, the syringe. It fills almost half the film's pages.

The loop closes

Six opening images come back in the last scene: the glass, the painting, the radio news, the alarm now a timer, the van, the phone. The dial stops at morning. The dashed arc is the next afternoon he does not wake up for.
