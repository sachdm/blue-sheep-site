# Log 01 — One Hundred Programmers (short film v1.0 → v1.1)

**1 Aug 2026. The short went through the gauntlet: one hundred iterations against a panel of festival-programmer lenses — Clermont-Ferrand, Sundance, TIFF, Berlinale Shorts, SXSW, Palm Springs, Tribeca, BFI, Sitges for the genre read, and the regional circuit that actually programs 17-minute two-handers. Notes converged fast; what follows is what survived.**

---

## What held, unanimously

- **The cold open.** Shari's hand, the glass, the van through the window. Nobody touched it.
- **"We're not supposed to be expecting you."** The single best line of dread in the piece. Several lenses wanted the whole film to grow from Joansy's careful kindness.
- **"Would you be happier dead?"** The turn. Untouched.
- **The magnet peel.** DRAIN DISPOSAL coming off to reveal SIDMAN'S RETIREMENT FACILITY read as the ending on every card. Untouched.
- **One apartment, one pub, one van.** The producibility note wrote itself.

## What changed (v1.1)

- **Runtime pressure.** The morning routine is the texture, but the panel has seen a thousand routines. Trimmed the lighter-box inventory and the grinder beat; the ritual survives at speed.
- **The talk show got sadder.** Cut Miles' on-air comeback. Now TV-Miles laughs along, delighted, while bar-Miles watches himself do it with no expression at all. The myth performs; the man observes. One shot instead of a scene.
- **Continuity: "This about the door?"** — a reference orphaned from the pilot's biker scene, which the short no longer contains. Now "This about my tab?" Three programmers caught it independently. Embarrassing. Fixed.
- **"Unkempt."** Caught in the pilot too. Fixed here.
- **Scene headings normalized** — every room in the apartment now carries MILES' APARTMENT, so the one-location economics are visible on the page.
- **The pre-lap V.O.** ("Waste of film and a waste of fish") referenced a boat we never see. Re-parenned as the voice of the photograph in the obituary — which is what it always was.
- **The ending bookends now close.** The reset room gains two lines: the painting straight (Shari's opening gesture), and the telephone square on its receiver — somebody put it back. The phone came off the hook during the interview and Miles didn't remember doing it. Now the room answers.

## Flagged and deliberately left

- **The two windows** (the family / the couple). Split panel — the conservative lenses wanted the couple gone, the rest called it the thesis: Miles watches other lives the way you watch weather. It stays.
- **"Based on the life of Hunter S. Thompson."** Not a craft note — a rights note. Flagged for counsel before any festival submission; several programmers will ask. The attribution is untouched here because that's a producer decision, not an editorial one.
- **Period ambiguity.** Rotary phone, radio, analog everything, no year. The panel read it as intentional timelessness. It is.
- **The motive question** — the obituary is already printed before Miles quits anything. The panel that pushed on this eventually landed where the film does: it was always tonight. The voicemail changes nothing, which is the point.

**v1.1 — script and PDF live on this page.**

## Addendum — v1.2 (same day)

Three notes from the re-rate closed the gap to the ceiling:

- **The door beat.** Between "It's tomorrow's paper" and the syringe, Miles now stands and crosses for the door — and finds a Michael already leaning there, not blocking it exactly, just there the way furniture is there. He sits back down on his own, because running would make it true. The capture is now chosen, not administered.
- **The McGarr call lost its second wind.** The Ryder Cup digression is gone; the column line survives. A page back in the pocket.
- **The autograph scene no longer competes with the TV.** The watching-father beat is cut; the kid, the blank stare, and "at least you're honest" carry it alone.
