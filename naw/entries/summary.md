# NOT ANOTHER WORD — Summary

**Logline.** A celebrated gonzo journalist, hollowed out by his own myth, tells an interviewer on camera that the world would be better off if he were dead — and the people sitting across from him agree, because they have come to arrange exactly that.

**Form.** 32 pages, 47 scenes. Three days in one apartment, structured as a ritual that repeats and degrades. Written by Sacha Mitchell and Cairns Nolan. Based on the life of Hunter S. Thompson.

---

## The shape

The film is a routine, played three times.

MILES GOODMAN wakes at three in the afternoon. Chivas before coffee. A cigarette from a wooden box. Cocaine out of a Yunnan dragon he was gifted on a mountain twenty years ago. The radio news. A day's worth of food eaten alone at the same booth in the same pub. Then the study, the typewriter, a tab of acid, vintage pornography looping on a VHS he doesn't watch, and writing until the cardinal arrives at the windowsill at dawn. Two Halcyon. Sleep.

**Day one** is the ritual intact, and it is almost enviable. He is famous enough that a father sends his son over for an autograph and Miles asks the boy, gently, to stay honest. He watches himself on a talk show being made the joke and doesn't mind. Then a biker puts him through the front door of Asters Pub and he lands on his back in the glass.

**Day two** the ritual holds and the work doesn't. The blank pages stack up beside the scrapped ones. He calls his agent's voicemail and quits everything — the book, the Paramount deal, the next project — and says he's retreating to Aspen. At the window, the cardinal does not sing.

**Day three** the world has quietly moved out from under him. His cleaner sees an unmarked white van across the street and makes a call. His bandage has been changed by hands that aren't his. Every apartment opposite has its curtains drawn. At Asters his booth is taken, the room is half empty, and Joansy — kind, sorry, unable to say why — turns him away. *We're not supposed to be expecting you.*

Then the interview crew arrives. A woman called Stacey Bradshaw and two men who are both called Michael.

## The turn

The interview is good. Miles is candid in the way only an exhausted man is, and he talks himself into the trap without help:

> *It would be much better if I died, so people could take the myth. I have no choice, I have to solve this problem... I have to kill off one life and start with another one.*

Stacey asks whether he would be happier dead. Then she hands him a newspaper and tells him to turn to page three, where his own obituary sits beside a photograph of him in Lombok. *It's tomorrow's paper.* The Michaels take a pulse and produce a syringe.

## The two endings

The script offers both and does not choose.

**Ending 1** stays outside. A gurney, a body bag, a van. The sliding door closes and reveals the logo: *Sidman's Retirement Facility.*

**Ending 2** goes in. Miles wakes in a grey welcome room facing a projector, and a recorded voice congratulates him. Sidman's collects important figures who have lost their tune, stages their deaths, and administers their legacies — the ultimate reward without the ultimate price. On the screen, a news anchor reports him found dead in his apartment of an apparent overdose. Then the door opens.

> *Hi Miles. It's good to have you with us. I'm Doctor Lance Sidman. Welcome to retirement.*

## The people

- **Miles Goodman** — late 30s. Brilliant, disciplined inside his own chaos, and finished. The myth has eaten the man and he knows it: *"I'm no longer necessary. I'm in the way."*
- **Shari** — the cleaner. Enters in the first image and the last ordinary one. Straightens the paintings, empties the ashtray, changes his bandage while he sleeps, and is the only person in the film who notices the van.
- **McGarr** — the agent on the other end of a phone Miles keeps talking past. Hangs up on him in act one; is a voicemail by act two.
- **Joansy** — the waitress at Asters. The film's warmest performance and the one who has to deliver its worst news without being allowed to explain it.
- **Stacey Bradshaw** — arrives as a journalist and leaves as an operator. Never raises her voice.
- **Sidman's Retirement Facility** — not a villain. A service, with a founder, a brochure and a chairman who says welcome.

## What it's about

A man says the myth would be better served by his death. An institution has been listening, agrees, and has the paperwork ready. The horror isn't that they kill him — they don't. It's that they take his own argument seriously and act on it, and that the legacy really will be better for it.
