# NOMAD — Log 13 · The Structure Pass (v3.7 → v4)

**diagnosis from the 37-beat map, and the five moves that answer it**

---

## What the beat map exposed

Mapping all 37 beats made four problems impossible to argue with:

1. **65% of the film was setup.** The engine — something is wrong with Cairns — didn't start until beat 25 of 37. In a 26-minute short the audience waited seventeen minutes for the story.
2. **Will was passive for the entire second movement.** Beats 11–18 happen TO him. He makes one decision in the whole stretch and says it as a joke.
3. **Cairns was never a person, only a screen.** He had nine pages of charm and then vanished into video calls. The curb was built to devastate, but the audience had known him for nine pages and never once seen him need anything.
4. **The game was charming before it was tragic.** Its first use was a bit. Its last use was a wound. Nothing connected them.

## The five moves

**1. THE 3 AM CONFESSION (new scene, p8).** John goes home. Cairns and Will alone on the carpet. Cairns admits he signed two weeks ago and never said it, because "saying it out loud makes it a thing I did instead of a thing that happened." Then he asks to be verified — *"Ask me the thing"* — and passes with the Wonderland story, messy and argued. And then, quieter, the actual ask goes past: **"You'd tell me. If it got weird down there. You'd be the one who could tell."** Will asks tell you what. Cairns waves it off. It's 3 AM.

This is now the hinge of the film. Will isn't a bystander who arrives too late — he's a man who was asked, in person, by name, and let it go. The game stops being a bit on first use and becomes the original sin.

**2. THE POOL CALL VERIFIES (p13).** A year on, an old reflex fires and Will runs the protocol mid-sell. Cairns passes — and gets his own story *wrong*, argues about it, makes it worse. "It gets worse every year, that's how you know it's true." It is the last time the story is ever told wrong, and the audience won't know that until Act Four.

**3. THE TELL IS REWIRED.** The likeness no longer recites the cubicle typo; it recites Wonderland — complete, every clause in the right order, from a video shot at 3 AM by a phone nobody was watching. "The real one never got it right. It was eleven, then twelve. On the ride, then before. The story moved every time he told it, because that was the point of telling it. This one recited it." The film's biggest reveal now pays a scene the audience watched in Act One.

**4. THE CURB CASHES THE CARPET (p26).** One full second of seeing him, and the film cuts the line back in: *"You'd be the one who could tell. Tell you what? Nothing, ignore me."* The cost is no longer abstract.

**5. THE GOOD DAYS COMPRESSED, JOHN BRAIDED THROUGH.** Market and limes-call merged; three beats saying "he's happy and oblivious" became two. John stops being two phone calls and becomes the chorus — "He's been in Oaxaca since March," then a voice note that goes unanswered: *"call him from a different number. Just, like, an experiment."* Every warning in this film is delivered by someone who loves him and shrugged.

## Result

29 beats. Cairns is a person. Will is culpable, not unlucky. The game runs plant → tragedy → inversion instead of bit → wound. The ending — John saying *prove it's you* to a dead floor with nobody left to answer — is unchanged, because it was always right.
