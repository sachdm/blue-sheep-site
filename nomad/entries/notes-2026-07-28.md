## The notes these came from

### 1. The three-call spine existed only in the summary

The summary states the design outright — Cairns sells Will, Cairns fails Will, "Will" sells John, *the frame teaches you to read it, then turns on you.* The script didn't do it. The third beat was a text message and the script explicitly never showed the screen, so the frame never turned on anyone. Highest-value fix in the draft, and it changed the ending's staging.

### 2. Will's blindness was the credibility problem

Established at the desk as elite — overturns a 98% machine verdict off three seconds, reading the eyes — then missed the tell four times and caught it instantly at the handshake. The instinct was right; motivated blindness is the true thing here. The fix wasn't fewer misses, it was making them active. We watch him do the work of not-seeing.

### 3. The tongue came from a different film

Best image in the script, wrong world. Nothing established mutilation, and it contradicted RETAINED FOR REFERENCE and the wall of lockers. The version that comes from this world is worse in the right way: a man who can say his own name and still can't prove it. It also finally dramatizes the logline's premise, which had been asserted and never shown.

### 4. Camila was a thesis, not a character

Two beats, then gone. Carrying the entire displacement argument alone, which is exactly what made her read as an obligation. Cut, function folded into the street.

### 5. The middle was passive

From storefront to render farm, things happened to Will and he reacted. The first time he acted was the two-window comparison, and it's the best scene in the script because it's the only one where he's the engine. The active-refusal pass helps; the deeper fix — moving the awakening earlier so the last third is a man hunting — is still open.

### 6. The premise was never dramatized

*Your verified digital likeness IS your legal self.* Gestured at once, in the final minute. Cairns on the curb now carries it.
