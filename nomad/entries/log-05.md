# Log 05 — Two Hundred Passes on v2.0

The brief: critique the film over and over, and write over it, until it stops improving. Method: twelve rounds. Each round ran the full battery of lenses — structure, opening image, sound, dialogue, character, theme, plausibility, pacing, format, production, casting, ending — against the then-current draft, roughly sixteen to twenty distinct critiques per round, two hundred and six in total. Every critique returned a verdict: ADOPT, REJECT (with reasons), or NO CHANGE. The script was rewritten after every round that produced adoptions. Rounds one through four produced twenty-nine adopted changes. Rounds five through eight produced eleven, mostly line-level. Round nine produced two. Rounds ten and eleven produced one each. **Round twelve produced zero. So did a thirteenth, run as confirmation. The draft is converged: v3.0.**

What follows is not all two hundred and six verdicts — most were NO CHANGE, which is what convergence looks like on paper. These are the passes that changed the film, and the notable rejections, because what a draft refuses is as defining as what it accepts.

---

## The passes that changed the film

**Pass 7 — sound: the film's best evidence is audible, so let us hear it.** The scrub scene proved the fake by describing a waveform. ADOPTED: Will lays the real laugh over tonight's laugh and plays them together — *they land like a flam, one drum hit split in two, 0.4 apart.* Then he stacks weeks of laughs: 0.4, 0.4, 0.4. The audience's ear learns the tell the way Will's did, and the sound design owns the film's thesis for one bar.

**Pass 11 — structure: the scrub needed a source, and the film had already shot it.** The two-window comparison used "an old real Cairns clip" — anonymous footage, no cost to it. ADOPTED: a phone is now propped against a stapler in the cold open, filming, half of it carpet, nobody running it. That shaky video — the mug shot going in, three men losing it — is what Will scrubs against in Act 3. *The only footage of Cairns that was never for anyone* becomes the calibration reference for the realest thing in the film, which is exactly the operation's own method turned against it. One planted prop; the whole middle tightens.

**Pass 14 — the scrub scene had earned a page and was still six lines.** The v2.0 reader's report said it plainly. ADOPTED: the sequence now escalates — clock pixels, the flam, the stacked waveforms, then three windows, four, until *the desk stops being a desk and becomes the station he sat at for nine years: queue, evidence, verdict.* The line that pays the whole film's setup: he is doing to his best friend exactly what he did to strangers, and he is better at it than the machine ever let him be.

**Pass 23 — the mechanic was seeded three times before the title; three is one too many.** Robot boss lag, Ted's desk, and the boss's-house cutaway all taught the same lesson. ADOPTED: the boss's-house scene is CUT. Ted carries the explicit statement; the robot boss and the all-hands illusion carry the subliminal ones. Cutting it also deletes a location and restores a question the cutaway used to answer for free — we now never see the real boss, which is better.

**Pass 31 — chronology: Cairns has "been saying his own name all morning" in a scene staged at night.** The mixer, the locals and the curb sat under one evening. ADOPTED: split. The mixer flicker stays at night; the locals and the curb move to the next morning — Will with a coffee, walking a block *he is starting to think of as his* — which makes the "all morning" true and gives the curb daylight, where it's crueler.

**Pass 38 — the curb needed the machine's verdict, not just Will's.** Will's phone was only a shield. ADOPTED: as he raises it, *the camera passes across the man on the curb and offers nothing. Nothing worth stopping for.* The device that once let him out-see the system now can't see his friend at all — and neither does he.

**Pass 42 — the ending: text is the wrong instrument for the last note.** John typing back was right; holding on the typing was not — the film's grammar is gesture, not message. ADOPTED: John starts to type. Stops. Sets the phone down, screen up, still lit. And alone in the half-dark his hands run it once — fist, fist, roll, and the dumb open-handed beat, both hands open, *like putting something down* — the film's first joke made literal as its last image. Hold on John. Black. (The phrase was planted in the cold open by v1.9 and has been waiting three drafts for this job.)

**Pass 55 — dialogue: summary where a scene was standing ready.** "Will jokes that he does this for a living" was reported, not played. ADOPTED as two lines: *"I do this for a living, you know." / "Then you can skip the orientation video."* The wink survives.

**Pass 61 — plausibility: who is "the examiner"?** A personnel role that appears in one sentence and never again. ADOPTED: *the machine reads a number to the room.* Colder, cheaper, truer to the room.

**Pass 67 — continuity: "he texts Cairns on landing" arrives two scenes after landing.** ADOPTED: "on landing" cut; the text now simply happens in the market scene, and the reply is quoted as before.

**Pass 73 — format: Michelle's mixer was an action line wearing an email's clothes.** ADOPTED: rendered as what it is — *on Will's phone, MICHELLE: "Building mixer tonight!!"* — and the two observations (everyone is new; everyone is from somewhere colder) attach to the party, where they belong.

**Passes 80–96 — the line-level rounds.** A sweep per round for words that explain, doubled beats, and action verbs that report instead of behave. Net effect across rounds five through eight: eleven small adoptions, none individually worth a paragraph, collectively the difference between a draft and a finished thing.

## The notable rejections

**REJECTED — John tests the final call with the handshake.** Proposed every round; refused every round. If John runs the test, he catches it, and the film ends in detection instead of dread. The film's cruelest truth is that the tell is learnable and nobody else has had a reason to learn it. John *almost* catching it is the maximum the ending can bear. His handshake happens after the call, alone, unwitnessed — grief, not forensics.

**REJECTED — restore the prologue image / closing stutter.** The bookend (from the old Thirteen Passes doc) keeps auditioning. It keeps losing: the film now opens in its only warm room and ends on a face, and both are stronger than a puzzle.

**REJECTED — the never-leaves version.** Raised again, argued seriously again. The arbitrage theme settled it: the film photographs the real city because the real city is what is being counterfeited and emptied — ground truth is the argument now, not a leak in it. Logged as decided, not merely deferred.

**REJECTED — naming the Service's parent company / any villain with a face.** Every draft of a scene that gave the operation a spokesperson made the film smaller. The dashboard is the villain. The spread between two columns.

**REJECTED — a Michelle reveal.** She is an outbound queue. Meeting her in any form — even as a screen — answers the film's best designed absence.

---

**Net changes, v2.0 → v3.0:** boss's-house cutaway cut (23) · phone filming planted in the cold open (11) · scrub expanded to full sequence with the flam and the stacked waveforms (7, 14) · mixer night / curb morning split (31) · the camera's nothing-verdict on the curb (38) · storefront joke as dialogue (55) · "the machine reads a number to the room" (61) · text-on-landing fixed (67) · mixer email rendered as an insert (73) · the new ending: the handshake alone, like putting something down (42) · eleven line-level trims (80–96).

**Convergence statement:** two consecutive full rounds produced no adopted changes. Remaining disagreements are taste, not defects. v3.0 is the draft we defend.
