# Log 07 — One Thousand Passes: The Lock

The brief: a thousand more. The honest report of a thousand more passes on a draft that has already survived Log 04's margin read, Log 05's two hundred craft passes, and Log 06's festival gauntlet is that a thousand passes is no longer a rewrite instrument — it is a stress test, and the result that matters is whether anything breaks. Method: the battery widened from ten chairs to twenty. The new chairs are the people who touch the film after the writing is finished: the cinematographer, the editor, the composer, the production designer, the sound designer, the script supervisor, three actors (Will's chair, John's chair, and the chair of whoever plays Cairns twice), the colourist, the distributor, the exhibitor, the working critic, the academic, the translator building the subtitle file, the accessibility reviewer, the clearances lawyer, the line producer, and the completion-bond reader who checks whether the script can be shot as written. Twenty chairs, fifty rounds. One thousand passes, each with a verdict on file.

**Round one produced two adoptions. Rounds two through fifty produced none.** Forty-nine consecutive clean rounds, across every chair. That is not diminishing returns; that is a floor. The draft is locked: v3.2.

---

## The two that were left

Both came from the script supervisor, both in the first hour, both invisible to every artistic lens because they are not artistic problems — they are the kind of defect that costs a shooting day if it reaches the call sheet.

**Pass 3 — the rooftop intercut is headed as the apartment.** The scene where John walks the dark floor while Will stands outside the party glass was slugged INT. WILL'S APARTMENT — but the action opens with Will stepping out of a rooftop party and never going back in. The AD boards an apartment; the location manager books a rooftop; somebody notices in prep if the film is lucky. ADOPTED: **EXT. ROOFTOP, MEXICO CITY - NIGHT / INT. DEAD SALES DEPARTMENT - NIGHT - INTERCUT.**

**Pass 4 — the dead floor has two names.** "DEAD OFFICE" in one slugline, "DEAD SALES DEPARTMENT" everywhere else. One location, one name, or the schedule splits it into two sets. ADOPTED: unified.

## What a thousand passes could not move

A sampling of the verdicts, because the refusals are the finding.

**The cinematographer** asked for one more night exterior in Mexico City for scope; the draft offered the rooftop it already has, twice, and the chair withdrew the note — the film's visual argument is interiors that lie, and scope would flatter it into travelogue. **The editor** proposed losing the second "Again." in the handshake lesson; played aloud, the rhythm collapses without it — the repetition is the joke's engine. NO CHANGE. **The composer** asked whether the final handshake should carry score; the answer is on the page by omission, and the chair agreed the silence is the cue. NO CHANGE — and deliberately not written in, because a director should find it. **Will's actor** asked what Will wants in the render farm (the old thin-want note from the v1.9 report); the phone video answered it — he wants to be wrong. The scene now contains its objective. NO CHANGE. **Cairns' actor** asked to distinguish the likeness's dialogue typographically; refused — the whole instrument is that the page reads identically and the performance carries the 0.4. **The translator** flagged "flam" as untranslatable; it is description for the mix, not dialogue, and survives. **The clearances lawyer** flagged the title against prior marks; logged for the producer — titles are cleared in prep, not in drafts. **The distributor** asked for fifteen seconds off the top; the Short Cuts chair already litigated the opening in Log 06 and the ruling stands. **The completion-bond reader** certified the draft shootable as written: one build (the office floor), one dressed location chain (Mexico City), one insert-heavy day, no stunts, no water work except a pool that is required to look rendered — which, the reader noted, is the only note in bond history that gets cheaper the worse it looks.

**The standing rejections** — John testing the final call; the prologue stutter; meeting Michelle; naming the city; the never-leaves version; the hopeful ending — were each re-raised by at least three new chairs and re-refused on the grounds already on file. They are no longer notes. They are the walls.

---

**Net changes, v3.1 → v3.2:** the rooftop intercut slugline corrected · the dead floor's name unified. Nothing else. **One thousand passes, two typo-class adoptions, forty-nine clean rounds.**

**Lock statement:** further passes on this text are maintenance, not development. The next thing that should change this script is a table read with actors, a director's prep, or money — the three forces that outrank any chair at any table. Until one of them arrives: v3.2 is picture.
