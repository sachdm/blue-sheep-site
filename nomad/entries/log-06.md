# Log 06 — The Festival Gauntlet: One Hundred Programmer Passes

The brief: put v3.0 in front of every chair at the programming table, one hundred times, until it converges — and it had better come out undeniable. Method: the same convergence discipline as Log 05, but the lenses changed rooms. No more craft department — this battery is the people who decide whether the film screens: the Short Cuts programmer four hundred films deep into a viewing season; the international programmer who reads subtitles for a living; the juror comparing it against nine other shorts at 11pm; the audience-award lens (what does a paying crowd do at minute six?); the sales agent; the accessibility/subtitling reviewer; the projection/DCP checklist; the opening-minute lens; the runtime accountant; and the last-image lens, because programmers admit they remember shorts by their final shot. Ten chairs, ten rounds, one hundred passes. The script was rewritten after every round that produced adoptions.

Rounds one and two produced the two changes that matter. Round three produced two trims. Rounds four through seven argued and adopted nothing. **Rounds eight, nine and ten returned no changes — three consecutive clean rounds. Converged: v3.1.**

---

## The passes that changed the film

**Pass 9 — the Short Cuts chair: your cold open ends on the wrong note.** v3.0's pre-title order was golf, chair joke, handshake, Ted, lights off — which sends the audience into the title card thinking about the mechanic (Ted's likeness) instead of the men. Four hundred shorts a season teaches you: the title card lands on whatever feeling immediately precedes it. ADOPTED: **Ted moves before the handshake lesson.** New order — the chair joke dies, they drift to Ted's glowing desk and watch a colleague who has already been replaced without anyone deciding it, and *then* Cairns teaches them the handshake. The lesson is now a response: the world is doing that, so we are building this. The cold open ends on "That's a good one," the lights click off on warmth, and the title lands on the thing the film is actually about — which is not software.

**Pass 14 — the international programmer: the elevator speech states what the cold open already staged.** "Nine years I ate lunch over a keyboard... my chair got promoted before I did" is a good line in a vacuum, and the film no longer needs it — the chair joke *is* that line, played instead of said, eight minutes earlier. Subtitled audiences feel redundancy hardest; they read it twice. ADOPTED: the rant cut to its keeper — *"You know what my rent is down there? A phone bill. I did the math on my life, and the math said leave."* The sell gets faster, and Cairns stops explaining himself, which he would never do anyway.

**Pass 22 — the juror: one sentence of narration is hiding in the action lines.** "Nobody else was looking for it, so nobody else saw it" — the all-hands has already shown us this: a floor of people not reacting while one man counts. The line is the film explaining its best silent beat. ADOPTED: cut. The lag, the frozen smile, and Will's face carry it alone.

**Pass 27 — the runtime accountant.** With the rant trim and the explainer cut, the read-through sits at approximately 16 minutes — inside the sweet spot where programmers can slot the film without sacrificing a second title. NO FURTHER CUTS ADOPTED: three separate attempts to trim the mundane John call, the market delights, and the height-marks beat were all rejected in later rounds — each is doing invisible load-bearing work (the friendship's pulse, the arbitrage played as charm, the cost of the apartment made physical). Shorter is not better past this point; it is only shorter.

## What the table refused to change

**REJECTED — cut the title card entirely (pass 31, the minimalist chair).** The card is load-bearing: it closes the *before* and prices everything after it. Films that are one unbroken sadness need no door; this one has a before and an after, and the door is where the audience steps through.

**REJECTED — open on the render farm, flash-forward (pass 38, the "grab them in ten seconds" chair).** The film's entire method is that wrongness arrives inside warmth. A cold-open flash-forward is the one edit that would make NOMAD like the four hundred other films on the pile.

**REJECTED — name the city Toronto for the co-production optics (pass 45, the sales agent).** The office floor is deliberately anywhere-north; specifying it buys financing conversation and costs universality. The two-pager can carry the Canadian angle. The script keeps its anonymity.

**REJECTED — subtitle the 0.4 flam beat with a visual readout (pass 60, accessibility chair, argued twice).** The waveform is already on screen in the scrub scene; the HoH audience gets the tell visually there. Adding a readout to the *final call* would hand John's almost-catching-it to the audience as text. The mix note stands: the flam must be felt in the room. Caption file gets "(laugh echoes, fractionally doubled)" — the craft solution, not the script one.

**REJECTED — a beat of hope for the audience award (pass 73).** The proposal: John, mid-handshake in the final image, stops — implying he knows. Rejected on the same grounds as every previous round: the film's last gift is grief that doesn't know it's grief yet. The audience that votes for endings votes for earned ones.

**REJECTED, standing — John tests the final call; the prologue stutter; meeting Michelle; the never-leaves version.** Re-raised by new chairs, re-refused for the reasons on file in Log 05. The rejections are now part of the film's architecture.

---

**Net changes, v3.0 → v3.1:** Ted repositioned ahead of the handshake lesson, so the cold open ends on the men and the title lands on warmth (9) · the elevator rant cut to the math line (14) · the all-hands narration line cut (22) · runtime settled at ~16 minutes with further trims explicitly refused (27).

**Convergence statement:** three consecutive clean rounds across all ten chairs. Every change the table could agree on is in; everything it split on was refused for cause and logged. What remains between this draft and any given programmer is taste — and taste is what festivals are for. v3.1 is the festival cut.
