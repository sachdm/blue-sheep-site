§ 01  —  Front page

An independent studio making documentary, narrative, and commercial work

The rest of that sentence is further down the page.

Blue Sheep is a production company in Toronto. We handle development, production and post for independent shorts, commissioned work and music films — a small crew, a long look, and a refusal to hand over anything we would not sit through twice.

What follows is not a reel. It is an index: eight finished titles, each with a year, a category and a note on what it was and why it looks the way it does.

01

Just Another F*cking Short Film

The first one. Shot fast, cut close, and unembarrassed about being small.

The title is the argument. A debut that refuses the ceremony of debuts — no establishing grandeur, no apology for the budget, nothing in the frame that had to be borrowed to look expensive. It existed to answer one question: could the company finish something.

Debut · Restraint · Handheld

02

Statuette

Chamber scale. One object, one room, and everything nobody will say out loud.

An object stands in for a person’s idea of themselves. Built around what a room does to a conversation when the thing on the table still has no name. Two people, one prop, and a running time short enough that nothing can be explained twice.

03

Runaway Sheep

The house film. Named after the mark on the door and made with the same instinct.

Leave, look back, keep walking. Loose in the shoulders and exact in the cut — the film we point at when somebody asks what the company actually sounds like rather than what it says it does.

04

DBC

Commissioned. A brief, a clock, a budget — held to the standard of the shorts.

Commercial work is not a lesser register here. Same crew, same insistence that the frame do more than deliver the product. A brief arrives with a clock attached; the only interesting question is what you protect once the clock starts.

05

Red Hand

Guilt as a genre problem. Colour doing structural work, not decoration.

Where a scene sits on the spectrum tells you how much the character already knows, and the film gives that away several beats before the dialogue does. Guilt treated as a genre problem rather than a mood — something with rules, escalation, and a shape you can cut to.

06

Until I Come Alive

The long gestation. A film about waiting, made slowly enough to be honest about it.

Held the longest of anything on the slate, and eventually the schedule became the subject. Patience is difficult to shoot; the only way through was to stop trying to make the waiting interesting.

07

Lost Puppets

Marionette logic applied to adults who have just noticed the strings.

Who is holding the strings, and what happens in the half second after somebody looks up and works out that somebody is. Everything built around it — the blocking, the rooms, the length of every take — is scaffolding put up to make that land.

Control · Strings · Forthcoming

Family Ties x Evo Da Saint

Collaboration. Made with a musician, on the musician’s terms.

Performance and portrait inside the same take. Working to somebody else’s tempo meant giving up the shot list more than once, which turned out to be the point rather than the cost.
